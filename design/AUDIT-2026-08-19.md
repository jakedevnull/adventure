# Canon Consistency Audit — 2026-08-19

Scope: `DESIGN.md`, `UNIVERSE.md`, `WRITING-GUIDE.md` (design/ directory, main @ 8748863).

Method: nine independent auditor agents — one per document pair (DESIGN × UNIVERSE,
DESIGN × WRITING-GUIDE, UNIVERSE × WRITING-GUIDE) and one per theme (time mechanic,
Illuminations, Awakening timeline, Travelers/multiplayer, tone & voice,
canon/extensibility) — each hunting for contradictions and underspecified rules.
42 raw findings were deduplicated to 30 distinct issues; each was then put to a panel
of three skeptic agents with distinct lenses (evidence verification, charitable
reading, materiality), instructed to refute. A finding survives only if a majority
could not refute it. **8 findings survived; 22 were refuted.** Findings that merely
restate the acknowledged open questions in DESIGN.md §9 were excluded by design.

---

## Confirmed findings

### 1. Item ownership and gifting across per-player stories is undefined
**Underspecified · High · survived 2/3 skeptics**

Gifting is a launch multiplayer feature (DESIGN §3.3 "Shared: … item gifting"), but
quest and puzzle state are per player ("If Petra has already opened the reliquary in
her story, it is still sealed in yours"). The docs never say whether room items are
per-player instances or shared world objects, whether a gifted story-critical item
advances the recipient's Thread, or what happens to items *dropped* in a shared room
(only *held* items are protected by the griefing posture). Petra can gift you the
reliquary's unique item while your reliquary is still sealed — duplication and puzzle
bypass in one move, strip-mining exactly what the personal-script rule exists to
protect. A Thread author cannot know whether gating a puzzle on possessing an item is
safe, and §9 does not track this.

*Dissent:* one skeptic held that "the worst a stranger can do is talk at you" is a
design commitment that forecloses gift-bypass, leaving only implementation detail.
The majority view: the invariant is stated but no mechanism is specified, and two
launch features collide.

### 2. Thread-critical visual content vs. the "fully playable blind" guarantee
**Contradiction · High · survived 2/3 skeptics**

DESIGN §6.1 licenses Illuminations to be "critical to a Thread — they are game, not
garnish." §6.2 guarantees "the game remains fully playable blind, over a screen
reader" — but the stated basis for that guarantee (every hotspot maps to a typeable
command) covers *interaction* parity only, not *information* parity. If the
Thread-critical clue is what the picture shows (a symbol, a word drawn in tiles, a
layer revealed by game state), no rule anywhere requires a textual equivalent or
forbids visual-only clues. One author will build image-dependent puzzles in good
faith; the accessibility guarantee silently breaks.

*Suggested fix is one sentence:* an Illumination's Thread-critical information must
also be perceivable in text (EXAMINE, or the room description).

### 3. The starting era of a new player is never stated
**Underspecified · Medium · survived 3/3 skeptics (unanimous)**

The Turning House is "the first room … the place every player draws their first
breath" (UNIVERSE §5) — but it exists in all six landings, and no doc says which era
a new player begins in, or whether players have a native/home era at all. The
first-run experience, the PAST/FUTURE tutorial, NPC reactions to a new Traveler, and
the melancholy theme's "from where you stand" all depend on this choice. Two writers
drafting the opening would pick different eras with no way to arbitrate. Not tracked
in §9.

### 4. PvE danger, combat, and death are undefined — yet the writing guide teaches menace
**Underspecified · Medium · survived 2/3 skeptics**

WRITING-GUIDE Rule 5 is entirely about delivering danger ("You are likely to be eaten
by a grue") and the calibration table models a troll encounter. DESIGN excludes
combat only from *multiplayer* launch scope (§9 item 7: "no PvP, no combat") and says
the game is "not a MUD with combat and levels" — but never states whether
single-player content may include hostile NPCs, player death, or any failure state.
One contributor will author deadly encounters the guide's style sanctions; another
will believe combat is out of scope entirely. The craft gate ("puzzles fair, Threads
completable") gives reviewers no basis to adjudicate.

*Dissent:* one skeptic noted the calibration table rewrites "a troll attacks you"
into "a troll blocks the way," deliberately removing the attack — arguably modeling
menace-as-atmosphere, not combat.

### 5. 64-cell Illumination width never reconciled with the phone-terminal pillar
**Underspecified · Medium · survived 2/3 skeptics**

Illuminations may be 64 cells wide, "aligned to the terminal's character grid so that
image and text share one typographic rhythm" (§6.2). Pillar 5 and §3.1 require the
same transcript to be a pleasure on a phone, where a readable monospace line is far
narrower. A 64-wide Illumination cannot stay grid-aligned on mobile without shrinking
relative to the text (breaking the shared rhythm) or side-scrolling (breaking the
transcript). Authors composing at 64 wide and at 40 wide are building to incompatible
canvas assumptions; the docs give neither a safe width nor a scaling rule.

*Dissent:* one skeptic proposed that 64 (well under the classic 80) *is* the mobile
accommodation. If so, the docs should say it.

### 6. Cross-Thread references collide with multiple endings and per-player state
**Underspecified · Medium · survived 2/3 skeptics**

Canon content is "citable by later Threads" (§7.2) and Threads "may *reference* other
Threads' outcomes" (§5.2) — but Threads have multiple endings and quest state is per
player (§3.3). Which outcome does a later Thread cite: an author-designated canonical
ending, or the outcome this player actually reached — and what if the player never
played, or abandoned, the referenced Thread? The two plausible readings produce
materially different content, and the craft gate's "Threads completable" check can't
be applied to outcome-dependent Threads without an answer.

*Dissent:* one skeptic read §5.2's italicized *reference* as allusive name-dropping
only (the "Marvel/Tolkien pleasure of recognizing a name"), never a dependency — a
plausible reading the docs could make explicit.

### 7. The provisional-noun flagging mechanism is broken, and the two lists disagree
**Contradiction · Medium · survived 2/3 skeptics**

DESIGN §8 says "the universe bible brackets the ones still open to change," and the
bible's header promises "Marginal notes in *[brackets]* flag names and facts that are
provisional" — but the bible's body contains no bracketed notes at all. Separately,
§8 lists *the Awakening*, *the Gap*, and *Travelers* as proposals, while §9 tracks
only *Threads*, *Illuminations*, *Foundry*, *Apocrypha* as provisional — and §9
declares the Awakening settled canon, which §8 calls a proposal. A contributor cannot
determine from the docs which core nouns are settled.

*Dissent:* one skeptic found via git history that brackets existed in earlier drafts
and were removed as items settled — so an unbracketed bible coherently means "nothing
currently flagged." The §8 ↔ §9 disagreement stands regardless; §8 is stale.

### 8. The object template violates the guide's own one-sentence rule
**Contradiction · Low · survived 2/3 skeptics**

WRITING-GUIDE's object template is labeled "one sentence, one true detail" but the
exemplar is two sentence-units ending in a punchy fragment ("The bell's clapper has
been wired still. Recently.") — the very device §5 rations to "once a realm, at
most," and the checklist caps objects at ≤ 1 sentence. A writer copying the guide's
own exemplar fails the guide's own checklist. Both upholding skeptics downgraded
severity to low: §5's ration bounds the practical harm.

## Refuted findings (22)

For transparency, the issues raised and killed by the skeptic panel, with the gist of
the refutation:

| Finding | Why it was refuted |
|---|---|
| "Every lettered age" debates the Lapse vs. "first scholars" in 2099 AA | "First scholars *of the Awakening*" is a scholarly-discipline claim; folk argument in every lettered age is compatible. |
| Temporal exits: rooms announce them vs. finding them is a craft | Announcement happens *in* the room; the craft is finding which rooms are loose. No conflict. |
| Turning House founding is a fixed point with no content | The House's existence, continuity, and family line are the content; the withheld date is deliberate mystery, and founding lore is studio-owned anyway. |
| Arrival template's "century of rain" implies wrong stride length | Figurative transit imagery in the guide's established register, not a mechanical claim. |
| Parser errors "specified in the writing guide" but never specified | Rule 6, the calibration table, and the checklist *are* the specification. |
| Per-realm style budgets unenforceable in per-contribution review | The budgets are hedged calibrations of hard per-passage rules; the craft gate already reviews realm context. |
| "Never past tense" vs. cross-era grammar | Rule 1 governs the narration frame; the guide's own exemplars model past-tense history clauses inside it. |
| "Battery-powered brass" vs. no-Earth-tech | It's a quoted Zork exemplar teaching an adjective principle, in a guide that frames all quotes as Zork source text. |
| Wonder-tech band never mapped to landings | Inferable: the everyday band's ceiling (printing, canal locks) verbatim matches 1099 AA; sketches are explicitly "canon in mood, not in every particular." |
| Machine-tell ban catches literal nouns (a real tapestry) | §5 supplies its own test: consultant *usage* is banned; a literal tapestry is information. |
| Cross-time co-op contradicts per-player state | §3.3's "Shared" bullet explicitly includes cross-millennia co-op as the stated carve-out. |
| BA calendars count down to an unknown event | The docs deny knowledge of *what* the Awakening was, not the dating scheme; the Hush shows the Before feeling it coming; unexplained temporal patterning is canon (the Ninety-Nines). |
| Gap width both fixed and unknown | Authorial canon (1000-year stride) vs. in-world epistemics ("nobody can prove") — deliberately distinct, stated together in §9 item 8. |
| Cross-era presence/simultaneity undefined | "For as long as she holds it" defines real-time simultaneity; the rest is below the doc's stated altitude ("a design, not an implementation plan"). |
| Illumination density denominator (place vs. place-era) | §4.4 defines "a room is a *place*"; era-variants are consistently "faces"/"era-states." |
| State-reactive Illuminations vs. append-only transcript | Per-cell animation already establishes Illuminations as live in-place elements; renderer detail is out of scope. |
| No fiction for players surfacing/de-surfacing | UNIVERSE §3 supplies it ("may pass without crossing"); the Petra line is one voice example, not the only sanctioned event. |
| "Each walks the years alone" vs. companions | The clause is bound to the carry prohibition (people no, objects yes), not a ban on co-striding. |
| Symmetry ban vs. the time-step template's contrast | The ban is on symmetry "for its own sake"; the template's contrast is information, which is §5's own test. |
| NPC era color vs. pastiche ban | Era color = nouns and idiom, per §4's own definition; the pastiche-grammar ban applies to everyone. |
| No sanctioned channel for the mandated melancholy | The channel is demonstrated: "An oak stands here. In a younger year, you planted it." — world-fact melancholy, narrator dry. |
| Apocrypha's relation to the three gates undefined | Derivable: canon content = "passed review," review = the gates; Apocrypha is the pre-review sandbox by definition. |
